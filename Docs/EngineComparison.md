Godot, Unity, and Unreal all had similar workflows. Moving, scaling, and editing objects were all fairly similar. Controls were also about the same. The way to use imported FBXs was also the same, just drag and drop into the folder/filing system, then drag and drop into the world. Done. Although Unreal required self-applying textures. Unity and Godot were auto-applied.

A difference between them all is the editor; obviously, Unreal’s editor was so much more complex and in-depth than the other engines. At least for what I had to do for this assignment, it was much more complicated to create things in Unreal. The menus are also much more overwhelming. Unity was okay, and Godot was bare-bones. Although the flow of things was the same, the options you had while in the workflow felt vastly different. Unreal has everything laid out for you, like everything. Unity has the important stuff laid out, more intensive things are hidden, and Godot felt like everything was a search mission. Once everything was found though, it was alright.



Prefabs vs Blueprints vs Scenes.

Honestly, it all felt the same. They all had their own screens to edit in, all had a content folder to drag and drop objects, and all could live update into the Main scene. The only difference I noticed was the naming convention. At least for this assignment, nothing was really different. Make a prefab, blueprint, or scene, then drag and drop assets into it, then save and drag it from the contents folder to the main level. 



I would probably choose Unity or Godot, as the GUI and menus were not as overwhelming and didn't throw everything in my face all at once. Godot’s simplicity is appealing, but Unity feels more polished.
